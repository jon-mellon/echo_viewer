# Staging UI/UX audit — 6 November 2026

Reviewed the live staging site at https://www.jonathanmellon.com/echo_viewer_staging/ against staging commit `05744d0`. This is a qualitative review, not a usability study or comprehensive accessibility certification. The walkthrough covered the landing page, cause/outcome selection, the education → income inference map and variable review, sleep exploration, search and empty states, filters, graph and table views, evidence detail, the schema workspace, and variable creation through the initial concept-selection screen. Desktop and a 390 × 844 viewport were inspected; the mobile viewport was simulated with a mouse, not a physical touch device. Connecting to OpenRouter and completing AI-assisted creation, publication, import/export, account and destructive flows were not exercised end to end.

## Fixed in this branch

- The landing screen visually covered the underlying workspace, but its controls were still in the accessibility tree. Returning home after a study exposed the previous anchors and variable controls behind the start page. The workspace is now hidden while home is active.
- The mobile schema workspace covered the analysis panels while their controls remained exposed to assistive technology. Those panels are now hidden while the mobile schema workspace is open.
- In the inference variable list, long labels were clipped with an ellipsis in narrow fine-pointer layouts, making similarly prefixed variables hard to distinguish. Labels can now wrap.
- A variable picker with no matches displayed only a clear button, without explaining the empty list. It now reports the empty or still-loading state, and avoids claiming no matches when a saved variable matches. A matching constituent concept is also included in the result's accessible name.
- A paper threshold that removed all links said “Nothing causes sleep,” even though this was only a filtered search result. An evidence-load failure could also be masked by that message. The empty state now describes what was found, and load failures take precedence.
- The paper threshold field was too narrow to display a five-digit value; its input is wider. With a lone node, automatic fit could zoom the graph into a huge unlabeled shape. Automatic fit now has a maximum scale, while manual zoom remains available.
- In variable creation, source selection rebuilt the list and lost keyboard focus. The selected checkbox now retains focus. The source list also stopped silently at 60 entries, hiding later eligible variables unless searched; it now includes all eligible sources. The current creation step is exposed to assistive technology.
- A missing-name error appeared beneath the definition field and was associated with the wrong input. It now appears and is announced with the name field, while definition guidance remains in place.
- Moving from the description to concept selection, or back to sources, left focus on a control hidden by the transition. Focus now moves to the new step. A warning before replacing AI choices no longer refers to a nonexistent “Choose manually” undo control.
- Returning to the source step and continuing with the same sources discarded selected concepts and renamed residuals. Those choices now survive an unchanged-source round trip, and the source step warns that changing sources clears them. Closing or replacing a draft also no longer deletes its saved AI checkpoint, so unfinished work can still be offered for resumption.

## Recommendations, in priority order

1. **Make the first result interpretable before presenting the entire network.** Education → income opened with 67 potential confounders and a dense, overlapping graph. At phone size, fitting the complete graph made the labels unreadable. Start with a summary and a manageable list or a focused neighborhood, and let users expand the map deliberately. Explain which filters are active and offer a clear route to the complete graph. Test this with actual research tasks before selecting a default; hiding edges without an explicit indication could mislead.
2. **Explain what an edge and its evidence do—and do not—mean.** The landing page promises what literature says about cause and effect, and the sleep list labels categories as causes. The mental health ↔ sleep detail includes records with different populations and strategies, including “implied selection on observables,” alongside randomized experiments. Present links as research claims or candidate relationships, distinguish study design and uncertainty near the result, and provide a concise explanation of extraction, coverage, and limitations. Avoid implying that a count of papers establishes a causal effect.
3. **Connect counts, directions and units.** For sleep, mental health showed 16 papers in the list; opening it showed 45 evidence records, including 21 in one direction and 24 in the other. Multiple records can belong to one paper, but that relationship and the inclusion of both directions are not explained at the transition. Label counts explicitly as distinct papers or findings, retain the requested direction as the initial focus, and explain the reverse direction and filtering.
4. **Rework evidence inspection for small screens.** At 390 px the evidence table initially showed only the first columns, with the target concept cut off and claim, strategy, population and decision farther to the right. The horizontal overflow was not obvious. Consider vertically stacked finding cards or a clearly signposted horizontal scroller with persistent context; keep the paper title and direction visible while inspecting a finding.
5. **Make the review workflow legible and navigable.** The inference list presents dozens of alphabetically ordered “Unaddressed” menus with little context about which variables deserve attention or what each disposition means. Add a short explanation, progress counts, search/filter affordances that are easier to discover, and an explicit rationale for any ranking. Explain the distinction between selecting a row to inspect it and recording a disposition. Preserve access to the full set.
6. **Clarify terminology and scope at the point of choice.** The picker shows a number next to each broad variable but does not visibly say it counts underlying concepts; elsewhere the same material is called variables. Definitions use “UOA” without explaining it, and names such as “risk prefs” and “info/comm exposure” are abbreviated. Use consistent terms, expand abbreviations, and define the unit of analysis in context. Explain broad group membership before users infer that every underlying concept is interchangeable.
7. **Improve discoverability of task controls on mobile.** Causes/consequences, diagnostic toggles and table/graph options live in the menu while the separate Variables/Map controls stay visible. Users must discover the menu to change the analytical question or representation. Test a compact, labeled task control near the results and keep secondary project actions in the menu. In the schema workspace, the persistent Variables/Map tabs also suggest that they control the page currently on screen.
8. **Make concept exclusions and recovery explicit.** The variable detail uses a small red × per concept without visible text explaining the effect on the schema, map, or evidence. Explain the scope and persistence of exclusion, provide an accessible undo/recovery path, and make touch targets adequate. Verify behavior with real touch devices before changing the control.
9. **Make search results explain their scope.** Searching the results list for “exercise” also returned categories such as medication and mental health because their constituent concepts matched, but the list did not show why. Show the matching concept, distinguish a direct variable-name match, and state that the search filters the list while the adjacent map or table continues to show all results.
10. **Offer an efficient keyboard route through the picker and graph.** The variable picker can contain 110 groups, each with a choice and definition control, making sequential Tab navigation lengthy. Provide and test keyboard navigation and an announced result count. The graph is exposed as an image rather than navigable nodes; make the table alternative discoverable to keyboard and screen-reader users, and verify equivalent access to definitions and evidence with assistive technology.
11. **Clarify the table's concept count.** The sleep causes table shows “Concepts” beside the number of papers, but the count is the size of the whole category (for example, 10,116 medication concepts), not necessarily the number supporting the displayed relationship. Label it as the category's total and explain what the paper count covers.
12. **Simplify creation progress announcements.** In the desktop accessibility tree, the creation stepper announced its visual number or check mark twice for each step. The active step is now identified in the branch; test the remaining duplicate announcement with supported screen readers and adjust the decorative markers if reproduced there.
13. **Offer a clear path to creating a variable without an external paid service.** After choosing a source and writing a definition, a new draft reaches a disabled Save button and instructions to connect an OpenRouter account. On the inspected screen there is no visible way to choose individual concepts from scratch; an existing split is an alternative only when one is available. Explain the account, cost and data-sharing requirements before users invest in a draft, and consider a searchable manual selection and review path. The included list also shows names without individual edit controls, so verify how users can correct AI suggestions before making that promise. No OpenRouter connection or paid run was attempted in this review.
14. **Make source changes recoverable after reviewing concepts.** Returning to the source step and proceeding with a different set resets existing concept choices and residual names. The branch now warns about that reset, but consider preserving compatible choices or explicitly offering to keep a recoverable copy. Unfinished AI classifications are locally checkpointed; make their relationship to the changed draft and the route to resume them clear. This finding follows the source-handling code; a paid classification was not performed.

## Useful foundations

The two task choices have concrete examples, selected anchors remain visible, evidence links expose source papers, and the schema screen explains that changes are local and publishing is separate. The list and graph provide complementary routes through the data. Preserve these strengths when reducing initial complexity.

## Verification and limits

The complete `npm test` suite passed using the existing pinned Dagitty cache. Local browser checks verified visibility transitions and wrapping against the stylesheet, the single-node fit using the actual graph renderer, and the creation presenter's focus, progress, and name-validation behavior with a synthetic fixture. The focused full-application Playwright test timed out at startup because the local preview reported “Failed to fetch” and its published-schema registry request returned 404; it did not reach the new assertions. Live staging itself loaded and supported the walkthrough. No publication or deployment was performed.
